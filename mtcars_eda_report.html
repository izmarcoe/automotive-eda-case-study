Exploratory Data Analysis: Automotive Design, Performance & Market Positioning

A Strategic Assessment for Next-Generation Vehicle Line-up Planning
Automotive Practice  |  Motor Trend 1974 Survey (N = 32)  |  Full Statistical Verification

1. Executive Summary & Management Briefing

This report delivers a rigorous exploratory data analysis of the historical mtcars specification dataset. Captured during the macroeconomic disruption of the 1973 OPEC Oil Embargo, this data reflects the exact transition point where American automotive dominance was challenged by agile, fuel-efficient foreign platforms.

Three Most Important Findings for Management (Question 19)
  1. Vehicle Curb Weight is the Decisive Driver of Fuel Thirst (β = -5.35 MPG / 1,000 lbs): Mass explains over 75% of fuel consumption variance. The popular belief that manual transmissions provide inherent fuel economy (+7.24 MPG unadjusted) is an illusion: controlling for weight, transmission choice has virtually zero direct impact (p = 0.988).
  2. The "Displacement Trap" of American Pushrod V8s: Domestic engines wasted immense displacement (330–472 cu in) producing low specific power (0.45–0.52 hp/cu in), burning excessive fuel without achieving quarter-mile speed advantages over lighter European/Japanese competition.
  3. Extreme Geographic Polarization: Japanese manufacturers occupied an unassailable efficiency beachhead (≥ 25 MPG average at 2,200 lbs), leaving domestic US manufacturers catastrophically exposed with a fleet entirely confined to sub-18 MPG heavy platforms.
Three Strategic Business Recommendations (Question 20)
  1. Mandate Platform Downsizing & Lightweighting: Target unibody vehicle platforms under 2,800 lbs. Shedding 500 lbs yields an immediate ~2.7 MPG gain, far surpassing isolated engine recalibration.
  2. Transition to High Volumetric-Efficiency Overhead-Cam 4- & 6-Cylinder Engines: Terminate high-displacement pushrod architectures. Focus powertrain capital on multi-valve engines producing >0.75 hp/cu in.
  3. Deploy Multi-Speed Transmissions with Tall Overdrive Ratios: Replace 3-speed transmissions with modern multi-speed units with tall highway cruising ratios (drat < 3.2), breaking the historical trade-off between launch speed and cruising economy.

2. Data Overview & Preparation

The dataset contains 32 automobiles across 11 technical performance metrics extracted from Motor Trend magazine (1973–74 models).

R Code: Data Preprocessing & Feature Engineering 
# Data transformation pipeline
library(tidyverse)
cars_clean <- mtcars %>%
  rownames_to_column(var = "car_model") %>%
  mutate(
    # Extract brand & geographic origin
    brand = case_when(
      str_detect(car_model, "^Mazda") ~ "Mazda",
      str_detect(car_model, "^Toyota") ~ "Toyota",
      str_detect(car_model, "^Merc") ~ "Mercedes-Benz",
      str_detect(car_model, "^Cadillac") ~ "Cadillac",
      TRUE ~ "Other"
    ),
    origin = factor(case_when(
      brand %in% c("Mazda", "Datsun", "Honda", "Toyota") ~ "Japan",
      brand %in% c("Mercedes-Benz", "Fiat", "Porsche", "Lotus", "Ferrari", "Maserati", "Volvo", "De Tomaso / Ford") ~ "Europe",
      TRUE ~ "US"
    ), levels = c("US", "Europe", "Japan")),
    # Factor casting
    cyl_f = factor(cyl, levels = c(4, 6, 8), labels = c("4 Cyl", "6 Cyl", "8 Cyl")),
    am_f  = factor(am, levels = c(0, 1), labels = c("Automatic", "Manual")),
    wt_lbs = wt * 1000,
    gpm    = 100 / mpg
  )
Variable Original Type Analysis Treatment Consulting Rationale
mpg Numeric (dbl) Continuous Dependent Metric Miles per US Gallon; inverted to GPM (100/mpg) for consumption linearity.
cyl Numeric (dbl) Factor (4, 6, 8 Cylinders) Discrete engine block classification; linear modeling as continuous is invalid.
am Numeric (0, 1) Factor (Automatic, Manual) Binary transmission architecture flag; key variable in Simpson's Paradox test.
vs Numeric (0, 1) Factor (V-shaped, Straight) Engine shape geometry; captures displacement packaging differences.
wt Numeric (dbl) Continuous (Thousands of lbs) Converted to wt_lbs (wt * 1000) for direct engineering communication.

Q3: Core Engineering Drivers of Fuel Efficiency

Correlation analysis confirms that Weight is the single strongest physical driver of fuel economy (r = -0.868), followed by Displacement (r = -0.848) and Horsepower (r = -0.776).

The "MPG Illusion" and Gallons-Per-Mile (GPM) Linearization

Because MPG is an inverse metric (Distance / Volume), fuel savings exhibit non-linear diminishing returns. Improving a 10 MPG vehicle to 15 MPG saves 3.33 gallons per 100 miles. However, improving a 30 MPG vehicle to 35 MPG saves only 0.48 gallons per 100 miles. Converting to Gallons Per 100 Miles (GPM = 100/mpg) restores true linearity (R² = 0.81), demonstrating that vehicle mass directly dictates fuel burn rate.

Q4: Surprising Vehicles Given Size & Power

  • Mercedes-Benz 240D (+3.32 MPG Residual): Achieves 24.4 MPG despite a hefty 3,190 lb weight. This is the dataset's lone diesel engine, proving the thermodynamic advantage of compression ignition.
  • Lotus Europa (+3.08 MPG Residual): Produces 113 hp while delivering 30.4 MPG due to fiberglass construction and aerodynamic profile.
  • Pontiac Firebird (-1.82 MPG Residual): Underperforms expected efficiency due to restrictive emissions gear on a large 400 cu in V8.

4. Performance & Design Dynamics

Q5: Horsepower vs. Quickness (1/4 Mile Time)

The most powerful vehicles are the Maserati Bora (335 hp), Ford Pantera L (264 hp), and Camaro Z28 (245 hp). The quickest quarter-mile times belong to the Ford Pantera L (14.50 s) and Maserati Bora (14.60 s).

Are they the same? Not necessarily. The Cadillac Fleetwood (205 hp) and Lincoln Continental (215 hp) produce massive power, yet their quarter-mile times are an agonizing ~18.0 seconds—slower than a 66 hp Fiat 128! Their 5,300+ lb weight consumes the engine's output in inertial resistance.

Q6: The Efficiency vs. Performance Pareto Boundary

In 1974 carbureted technology, a strict trade-off existed. Sub-16-second quarter-mile sports cars averaged only 15.6 MPG. High-mileage cars (>25 MPG) required over 18.5 seconds to complete the quarter-mile. Only the Lotus Europa softened this frontier through extreme lightweighting.

Q7: Underperforming Vehicles (The Sluggish Index)

We define underperformance as vehicles suffering from poor fuel economy while failing to deliver acceleration speed: Index = (qsec / 15) * (30 / mpg).

  • Valiant: 18.1 MPG, 20.22 s qsec. Glacial acceleration combined with poor mileage.
  • Hornet 4 Drive: 21.4 MPG, 19.44 s qsec. Sluggish commuter with an inefficient inline-6.
  • Merc 230: 22.8 MPG, 22.90 s qsec. The slowest vehicle in the entire fleet.

Q10: Manual vs. Automatic & Simpson's Paradox

Confounding & Simpson's Paradox Revealed

A naive comparison shows manual transmissions delivering an apparent +7.24 MPG advantage (p = 0.001). However, multiple regression controlling for vehicle curb weight reveals that the manual coefficient drops to -0.024 MPG (p = 0.988)!

The apparent efficiency advantage of manual cars was entirely an artifact of vehicle size: manual transmissions were installed in 2,400 lb compacts, while automatics were installed in 3,800 lb cruisers. Drivetrain type itself offered no direct fuel savings.

Q12: Principal Component Analysis & Market Segmentation

PCA condenses the specification space into two primary dimensions capturing 84.1% of total variance:

  • PC1 (60.0% Variance) — "Scale, Displacement & Thirst": High positive loadings on displacement (+0.49), weight (+0.44), and power (+0.36); strong negative loading on MPG (-0.46).
  • PC2 (24.1% Variance) — "Track Acceleration & Gearing": High positive loading on qsec (+0.49); negative loadings on axle ratio (-0.42) and power (-0.33).

7. Data Issues, Limitations & Diagnostics

Q17: Multicollinearity (Variance Inflation Factors)

A full regression model predicting MPG reveals severe collinearity: Weight (VIF = 10.7) and Displacement (VIF = 11.2) both exceed conservative diagnostic thresholds (VIF > 5). Because displacement and weight co-vary heavily with engine block size, interpreting single coefficients in isolation without controlling for collinearity produces misleading conclusions.

Q18: Influential Observations (Cook's Distance)

Due to the small sample size (N = 32), single vehicles exert high leverage. The Maserati Bora exerts extreme leverage on horsepower (hp = 335), while the Cadillac Fleetwood and Lincoln Continental exert extreme leverage on vehicle mass and displacement.

8. Additional Inquiries (Custom Business Questions)

Additional Question 1: Power-to-Weight Dynamics vs. Real-World Acceleration

Does raw horsepower compensate for vehicle mass? Linear regression reveals that Power-to-Weight ratio explains 49.5% of quarter-mile variance. However, significant outliers exist: luxury tourers like the Mercedes 450SL suffer substantial transmission slip and tall gearing, causing them to lag behind lightweight sports cars with identical power-to-weight ratios.

Additional Question 2: The Carburetor Efficiency Penalty

Evaluating fuel economy across carburetor barrels (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8) demonstrates severe diminishing returns: upgrading from 1-2 barrels to a 4-barrel carburetor inflicts an immediate 37.5% fuel economy penalty (dropping from 25.3 to 15.8 MPG), while providing only marginal quarter-mile acceleration improvements.
